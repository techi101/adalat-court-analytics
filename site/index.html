District courts of India, NJDG snapshot of 26 Nov 2026 An independent analysis. Not made by or for Adalat AI.

India's district courts close fewer cases than they take in. Every year.

There are 5.27 crore cases waiting in district courts. The single most common thing those cases are waiting for is a witness to be examined. This page measures the backlog from the government's own data, then asks how much faster evidence recording could change it.

More cases come in than go out

In 2025 district courts took in new cases and closed . The difference, , was added to the pile. That has happened in every year NJDG reports, 2018 to 2026.

The gap is not huge next to the flow: courts close about cases for every 100 that arrive. That matters for the model further down. A tool does not have to double court output to stop the backlog growing. It has to close a few percent more.

The queue is longest at the witness box

Each pending case has a next hearing with a purpose. The most common purpose is witness examination: cases, about of every case whose stage is recorded.

This is the step Adalat AI's courtroom transcription speeds up. A magistrate quoted on its site says they could hear two to three witnesses a day before, and five to six after. Separately, NJDG lists "witness" as the delay reason on cases.

Three states, three different stories

Uttar Pradesh holds the largest backlog. Maharashtra is second and fell behind in every year shown. Kerala, smaller, closes roughly as many cases as it receives. National averages hide this, so the model below can be run for any state.

What if evidence were recorded twice as fast?

A simple throughput model. Only contested trials need witnesses, and only part of a contested trial's court time is evidence. So faster recording speeds up that part, in the courtrooms that have it. Every input is a slider, and the one that is my own guess is marked as a guess.

How this was made

Data

NJDG has no public API. A Python script opens the dashboard in a headless browser and calls the same requests the page makes when you click a tile, for India and for each of 36 states and union territories. The raw responses are saved untouched, then a second script turns them into the one JSON file this page reads.

Scope

District and subordinate courts only. High Courts and the Supreme Court have their own grids and are not included. Pre litigation cases (12.4 lakh) are excluded, as NJDG does in its totals.

The model

Speed up per contested case = 1 / ((1 − e) + e × before / after), where e is the evidence share. Extra closures = closures × contested share × coverage × (speed up − 1). It is a back of envelope throughput model, not a forecast: it ignores adjournments, judge vacancies and new filing trends.

Caveats I would raise first

The contested share is one month of national data. 2026 is a partial year. NJDG coverage has grown since 2018, so early years may undercount. Uttar Pradesh closed more than it received in 2025, but its 2026 filings so far are already 41% above all of 2025, so its "shrinking" result may not last.

Everything, including the raw snapshot, is on GitHub. Re run the two scripts to refresh.
